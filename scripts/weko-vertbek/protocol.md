# Reading protocol: the WEKO vertical-restraints audit

Frozen 2026-10-01, before any judge has run. If it proves incomplete, the
rule is changed here, the date is changed, and every label is made again. A
clarification is never added in the middle of a run.

## What is judged

One **proposition** of the Swiss vertical-restraints notice (Bekanntmachung
or Erläuterungen, a given version) against **eight passages** from eight
different decisions of the Swiss record: the Competition Commission (WEKO)
and its Secretariat, the federal courts, cantonal courts and a few other
authorities. The question for every passage is the same:

> Does this passage show the rule the proposition states being **used to
> decide something**?

## Five labels per passage

**applies**: the rule stated in the proposition is applied. It is held
against facts, a consequence is drawn from it, a party's or the authority's
conduct is measured by it, or the reasoning rests on it. The rule may be
applied without the notice being named, and in the decision's own words.

**partial**: only part of the proposition is applied, or the passage applies
a narrower or neighbouring rule that covers part of it. Length does not make
an application partial: one subordinate clause that draws a consequence from
the whole rule is **applies**.

**contradicts**: the passage decides the matter of the proposition by the
opposite rule, departs from the proposition, or holds it inapplicable where
it claims to apply.

**recites**: the passage restates or quotes the proposition, or lists it
among the applicable rules, and does nothing with it.

**unrelated**: the passage is about something else, including a different
rule of the same notice, the same words used for another legal question
(Geschäftsgeheimnis in a transparency case is not Know-how in a vertical
agreement), or ancillary restraints in a merger when the proposition is about
vertical agreements.

## Order of questions

Stop at the first yes:

1. Is the passage about the subject of this proposition at all? No → **unrelated**.
2. Is anything done with the rule beyond restating it? No → **recites**.
3. Does the decision go against the rule? Yes → **contradicts**.
4. Is only part of the proposition used, or a neighbouring rule? Yes → **partial**.
5. Otherwise → **applies**.

## Where it goes wrong

- **The consequence can be one clause.** "..., weshalb die Abrede als
  qualitativ schwerwiegend gilt" after a restated rule is applies.
- **Agreeing with the topic is not applying the rule.** A passage about
  selective distribution is not support for a proposition about the
  conditions of selective distribution unless it uses those conditions.
- **A court finding that WEKO breached the rule** is applies, not
  contradicts: the rule is the yardstick. Contradicts is kept for a decision
  that adopts a rule opposite to the proposition.
- **Dates and regimes are shown, not judged.** A passage decided before the
  version, or under the 1995 act, is labelled by what it does; the time axis
  is computed afterwards.
- **Statute text.** If the proposition repeats a paragraph of Art. 5 KG, a
  passage applying that paragraph applies it.
- **A provision that did not exist yet.** A decision under an earlier
  version of the cartel act cannot apply a paragraph introduced later (Art. 5
  para. 4 KG and direct sanctions date from 1 April 2004). If such a decision
  reaches a similar result through another provision, that is **partial**.
- **The window is cut.** Passages are windows of about 1,200 characters and
  may start or end mid-sentence. Judge what is visible; if what is visible is
  not enough to say, the label is **unrelated**, not a guess.
- **Redactions** ("[…]", "[...]") are business secrets removed for
  publication. Judge the reasoning around them.
- **Language.** Passages may be in French or Italian; the rule is the same
  whatever language states it. Quote in the language printed.

## The proposition's label

Derived, not chosen:

- **supported**: at least one passage applies it;
- **fragment**: none applies, at least one is partial;
- **recites**: none applies or is partial, at least one recites;
- **absent**: every passage is unrelated or contradicts.

A passage that contradicts never supports. Contradictions are counted
separately: practice departing from the notice is a finding of its own.

The reader marks the passages that carry the label ("stützt"); the judge
returns, for every passage that applies, is partial or contradicts, a
verbatim quote of the words that show it.

## What is deliberately not shown

Whether the decision cites the notice, how much of the proposition it
reproduces by the formal measure, and whether the item is a control. Those
are in the data, for measuring agreement afterwards.

## When unsure

Choose the weaker label (partial over applies, unrelated over partial). An
invented application costs more than a missed one: the second reading and
the other judges see the same passages, and the disagreement shows.
