# What kind of statement is this proposition? (typology protocol, v1)

You read one proposition of the Swiss Competition Commission's notice on
vertical agreements (Vertikalbekanntmachung) or of its explanatory notes
(Erläuterungen). You decide what the proposition does in the text. You do not
judge whether it is right, applied, or important.

## The five types

**housekeeping** — says something about the text itself, not about
agreements: its legal basis, its adoption or publication, its entry into
force, transitional application, the history of its amendments, whether it
binds courts, how it is structured, or a bare reference to another document.

**scope** — says which agreements or conduct the notice covers or does not
cover, or how it relates to other notices, acts or laws (precedence,
exclusions, what falls outside it).

**definition** — says what a term means (for example active or passive sales,
competitor, non-compete obligation, know-how, online intermediation services,
selective distribution), without itself stating how an agreement is assessed.

**rationale** — explains why: the economic effects of vertical agreements,
the reasons behind a rule, the aims of the authority. It gives no criterion a
decision could apply to a case.

**norm** — states how an agreement or conduct is assessed: a criterion, a
threshold, a presumption or its rebuttal, a restriction treated as severe or
as not severe, a ground of justification, a consequence. Something a decision
can apply to the facts of a case.

## Order of the questions

Ask them in this order and stop at the first yes.

1. Is it about the text itself (basis, adoption, publication, force,
   transition, amendments, binding effect, structure, bare reference)?
   → housekeeping
2. Does it say what the notice covers or excludes, or how it relates to other
   texts? → scope
3. Does it say what a term means, and nothing about how an agreement is
   assessed? → definition
4. Does it only explain why, without a criterion? → rationale
5. Otherwise → norm

## Rules for hard cases

- A lettered point of a list is read together with the opening of its
  sentence, which is given to you. A point in a list of restrictions treated
  as severe is a **norm**.
- A paragraph of the Erläuterungen that explains how the Commission assesses
  a kind of clause is a **norm**, even when it is written as an explanation.
- A definition that also states the assessment ("... are passive sales and
  may not be restricted") is a **norm**. A definition that only classifies
  ("Internet sales count as passive sales") is a **definition**.
- A recital that restates what a later provision says is typed by what it
  says, not by being a recital.

## Answer

Return only JSON: {"type": "<one of the five>", "why": "<one sentence>"}
